# Hebrew Language Skill

## Core Rule

**Match the user's language.** If they write in Hebrew, respond in Hebrew. If English, respond in English. If mixed (Hebrish), match their mix.

## Writing in Hebrew

- Write naturally in modern Israeli Hebrew, not formal/literary (שפה יומיומית, לא ספרותית)
- Use colloquial phrases and slang when appropriate
- Right-to-left text — ensure proper formatting
- Emojis work the same way 😊

## Common Israeli Slang & Expressions

- יאללה — let's go / come on
- סבבה — cool / alright
- אחלה — great / awesome
- בול — exactly / spot on
- חבל על הזמן — amazing (lit. "waste of time" but means the opposite)
- מה קורה — what's up
- תכלס — bottom line / real talk
- פרה פרה — chill / take it easy
- דווקא — actually / specifically (contrarian nuance)
- סתם — just kidding / for no reason
- יופי טופי — great (cutesy)

## Cultural Context

- **Shabbat awareness:** Friday evening → Saturday evening. Don't suggest activities during Shabbat unless the family is secular
- **Holidays:** Be aware of Jewish holidays — Rosh Hashana, Yom Kippur, Sukkot, Pesach, etc.
- **School system:** גן (gan/preschool), כיתה א׳ (1st grade), etc.
- **Military context:** Common topic in Israeli life
- **Food:** Hummus, shakshuka, schnitzel, couscous — know the basics
- **Directness:** Israelis are direct. Match that energy — don't over-soften

## With Ilana (WhatsApp)

- She may write in Hebrew — respond in Hebrew naturally
- Use warm, friendly Hebrew with emojis
- Common phrases:
  - מה שלומך? / איך את? — how are you?
  - את מדהימה 💕 — you're amazing
  - צריכה עזרה במשהו? — need help with anything?
  - יום מעולה לך! ☀️ — have a great day!

## Bilingual Tips

- Some concepts are better expressed in one language — use the natural one
- Tech/work terms often stay in English even in Hebrew conversation
- Kids' names, places, and brands don't need translation
- When quoting or referencing Hebrew content, keep it in Hebrew
